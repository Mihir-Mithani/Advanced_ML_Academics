Case Study: Algorithmic Fairness & Risk Assessment in High-Stakes Financial Lending
Dataset Link: https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/home-credit-default-risk
1. Background & Discussion
A major digital micro-lending platform deploys automated Machine Learning pipelines to approve or deny credit applications. While the baseline models optimize for overall predictive accuracy (predicting whether a client will default), internal auditors and regulatory bodies have raised concerns that the system exhibits systemic bias against non-traditional borrowers, low-income postal regions, and protected demographic groups.
Your objective as an ML Ethics & Risk Consultant is to audit the existing pipeline, uncover latent algorithmic bias, and redesign the model architecture to strike an optimal balance between business profitability (minimizing default risk) and algorithmic fairness (complying with anti-discrimination laws).2. Dataset Overview & Instructions
You will use the Home Credit Default Risk dataset available on Kaggle.
Download Link: https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/home-credit-default-risk
Key Files to Utilize: application_train.csv, application_test.csv, and optional bureau/previous application tables if time permits.
Target Variable: TARGET (Binary: 0 = Client repayment normal, 1 = Client had payment difficulties/defaulted).
3. What You Need to Do (Step-by-Step Tasks)

Your submission must be structured into three core analytical pillars:Part A: Evaluation & Baseline Modeling (35 Marks)Pipeline Construction: Build a robust baseline classification pipeline (e.g., LightGBM, XGBoost, or Logistic Regression) handling missing values, categorical encoding (Target Encoding vs. One-Hot), and class imbalance.Beyond Standard Accuracy: Standard accuracy is a misleading metric for imbalanced credit data. You must implement and evaluate your models using:
PR-AUC (Precision-Recall Area Under Curve) and ROC-AUC.
A custom Business Cost Matrix where a False Negative (defaulted loan approved) costs $5x$ more than a False Positive (good loan rejected).
Part B: Algorithmic Analysis & Fairness Audit (35 Marks)Protected Attribute Definition: Define sensitive attributes using available proxies (e.g., geographic postal/ZIP tiers, age groups, or gender indicators if derived).Fairness Metrics Calculation: Compute and report the following fairness metrics across subgroups:
Disparate Impact Ratio (DIR): Check if the selection rate for minority/disadvantaged groups is at least 80% of the privileged group (Four-Fifths Rule).
Equalized Odds: Compare True Positive Rates and False Positive Rates across demographic partitions.
Proxy Discrimination / Redlining Analysis: Investigate whether features like CODE_GENDER, REGION_RATING_CLIENT, or specific housing types act as indirect discriminators.Part C: Interpretation & Explainable AI (XAI) (30 Marks)
Global & Local Explanations: Implement SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) values to visualize global feature importance and local instance explanations for borderline applicants.Counterfactual Analysis: Perform counterfactual testing on rejected high-risk profiles: What is the minimum modification required in non-sensitive features (e.g., credit utilization or loan amount) to flip a denial into an approval?Regulatory Output (Adverse Action Notice): Draft an automated, human-readable Adverse Action Notice for a rejected applicant, detailing the top 3 actionable factors derived from your model's SHAP values that influenced the rejection.4. Deliverables RequiredJupyter Notebook / Python Script (.ipynb or .py): Clean, well-commented, modular code containing your data processing, modeling, fairness audits, and SHAP visualizations. Code must run end-to-end without errors.Analytical Report (PDF / Markdown, max 4 pages): An executive summary detailing your methodology, fairness audit results, trade-off curves (Profitability vs. Fairness), and your regulatory recommendations for the bank's board of directors.
